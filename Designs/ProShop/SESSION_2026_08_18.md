## 1. THE TERRAIN/PAINT CURSOR DID NOT FOLLOW THE MOUSE

**The ring was never the problem. The stability freeze was.**

At frame 900 of active walk the freeze takes every bit-stable transform in the
scene, and the editor's overlays qualify — they sit invisible and perfectly
still for the whole walk because nothing writes to them until they are revealed.
Frozen, they take every write to `position` and pass none of it to
`matrixWorld`. So `editorCursorState()` reported a ring tracking the mouse
exactly, while the renderer drew it where it stood fifteen seconds earlier.

Measured on your own save:

| | frozen build | fixed build |
|---|---|---|
| distinct ring positions over 5 pointer positions | **1** | **5** |
| offset from the pointer | up to **830 px** | **0 px**, all 15 |
| `matrixAutoUpdate` | false (`StabilityMatrixFreeze`) | true |
| objects still frozen | 3,117 | 3,100 |

**Why two earlier probes passed it.** Both projected `object.position` and
compared it against the mouse — which is circular, because that number came
from the mouse ray in the first place. `editorCursorState()` now reports the
DRAWN transform and whether the object may still move.

Two halves to the fix: the on-demand overlays (brush ring, falloff, fill,
feature preview, placement ghost, measure group, playtest ball, aim arc,
overview pin) are marked as a live visual hierarchy so the freeze never enrols
them; and the watchdog that thaws a surprise mover no longer ticks only while
`walk.active` — the editor is not walking, so anything a verb wrote to in the
editor stayed frozen all session.

Evidence: `qa/editor-follow/watchedfail.json` vs `fixed.json`, frames viewed
both ways, and a continuous sweep clip at `qa/clips/cursor/tiles-13.png`.

## 2. ONE PURCHASE EMPTIED THE LINE

**Two faults, and the second was invisible until the queue was made to say why
anybody left it.**

**The patience fuse never credited the line moving.** One clock, seconds since
this customer's route turned for the counter, with nothing ever resetting it.
Five shoppers who arrive together expire together, and the only thing holding
them is their queue index — positions 0 and 1 are unconditional, everyone behind
is not. Complete a sale, the head is spliced out, every index drops by one, and
the customer newly exposed at position 2 is carrying a fuse that burned through
minutes ago. They walk. The array shifts again. The next walks.

That clock runs at four times the wall, so `PATIENCE_FULL`'s ten authored
minutes are **two and a half real ones** — about one unhurried transaction.

There are two clocks now: `wait` since the line last advanced, reset on every
advance, and `total` in the line at all, on a fuse three times as long. Being
served is itself progress, which also removes a one-frame race where somebody
expired just before the advance that would have saved them.

The negative control ships with the test — the old rule replayed in the same
harness sends **3 of 5 out inside one second**, leaving one person in a shop
that had five. That is your bug report, in a unit test.

**And the navigation was throwing people out of the line.** A five-deep staged
queue lost two customers with their patience clocks barely started. The new
queue-exit log named it: `nav-gave-up` — rung 5 of the stuck ladder, which
abandons the stop. At a counter that means being thrown out of the LINE by the
navigation, which from behind the till is a customer silently deciding not to
buy anything. A queue is the one stop that fixes itself, so rung 5 there now
holds instead of abandoning, and the queue slot is validated at assignment the
same way a browse stop is — the coverage gap Goal 38 wrote down and could not
evidence at the time.

In game, on your save (`qa/queue-exodus/clean.json`): five deep, clocks at
380.9 s, the head cleared, **all four credited an advance and their clocks
reset**, zero give-ups. `qa/clips/queue-fixed/tiles-49.png` shows the four still
standing at the till eight seconds later.

**NOT PROVED, and I am not claiming it:** a completed tender. The harness drove
scanning and the card to `card-entry` and cash to `cash-drawer` but could not
close either — the monitor draws no action rows at those stages — so the
line-advance half is measured through `dismissCounterCustomer()`, the same
`removeCustomer → leaveQueue → every index drops` funnel a sale uses. It is not
a sale. Logged in HARNESS_DEBT §11.

## 3. THE LAPTOP BAR

**It was fast-then-slow by construction.** Nine tenths of the bar in a fixed
480 ms, then an asymptotic crawl through the last tenth with a 900 ms time
constant. A sprint and a crawl, at any speed.

| | tenths of the bar, in ms | unevenness |
|---|---|---|
| before | 64, 40, 49, 58, 50, 51, 58, 50, 58, **0** | **64x** |
| after | 46, 46, 54, 48, 47, 50, 51, 50, 50, 38 | **1.42x** |

One straight ramp across 97% of the bar, over a duration THIS MACHINE has
actually produced: the estimate starts from the choreography's own beat (main.js
hands it over rather than clubhouse.js keeping a second copy), is re-estimated
by EWMA after every open, and is remembered across sessions — because the open
you notice is the first one of a session.

**And the half a bar cannot fix.** Three of eight first-opens **blocked the main
thread for 2.3–3.2 seconds**. No frames ran at all, so the bar was frozen
wherever it had got to, which describes "takes 5 seconds" far better than any
curve. It mints nothing — zero new programs, geometries or textures across the
stall, no long-task entries — and I have not diagnosed it. It is intermittent on
the same build and the same save.

The instrument fell into its own trap first: with no frames every tenth measured
0 ms and the evenness thresholds sailed through. It now fails loudly.

## 4. NPCs RUNNING INTO EACH OTHER

**I tried the thing you asked for, measured it, and it made things worse.**

Handing ORCA each neighbour's INTENDED velocity — heading times speed, after the
look-ahead has turned it — instead of the displacement they achieved last frame.
Same save, same staging, four minutes each, one boot apart:

| | velocity (kept) | intent (rejected) |
|---|---|---|
| frames touching | 2 | **14** |
| frames interpenetrating | 0 | **4** |
| closest approach | 0.6825 yd | **0.5364 yd** |
| contact episodes | 1 (0 hard) | **5 (2 hard)** |
| infeasible solves | 2.15% | **26.2%** |

The reason is in the derivation. ORCA's half-plane is built on the RELATIVE
velocity of a pair and its reciprocal split assumes the neighbour is currently
travelling at the velocity you were given. A preferred velocity is one they have
NOT achieved — their own solve is about to reduce it — so every agent
over-estimates how fast the other is closing and falls through to the infeasible
path twelve times as often. The anticipation was already there: `timeHorizon` is
2.0 seconds of guaranteed contact-freedom, which IS knowing what the other is
about to do. Reverted, with the numbers written into the code so nobody tries it
a third time.

**What did help was the queue work.** Same driver, same staging, one boot apart:

| | without the nav fixes | with them |
|---|---|---|
| people | 7 | 8 |
| frames touching | 5 | **2** |
| frames interpenetrating | 1 | **0** |
| closest approach | 0.6099 yd | **0.6825 yd** |
| contact episodes | 2 (1 hard) | **1 (0 hard)** |

**And a caution.** Under this staging both builds are near-clean — 2 touching
frames in 17,500. That does not match "all the time", so either the stress that
produces it is different from what I staged, or what you are seeing is not
body-against-body. The two candidates I did not test: **the player being walked
into** (you are a neighbour the solver does not expect to yield), and the queue
under a deeper line than eight.

## 5. A COPY OF THE GREYBOX WITH THE NEW ASSETS — `pine-hills-v3`

Reachable as `--clubhouse=pine-hills-v3`, or from the Developer tab.

It is a PRESENTATION, not a new room. v3 resolves to the same layout constant as
v2, so every datum is shared: measured in Electron on both rooms, the counter,
the register stand, all six queue slots and the public room bounds come back
**byte-identical**. Nothing dressed in v3 can move a stand point, a collider or a
queue slot out from under the variant it is a copy of, and a test holds that no
datum may branch on v3 at all.

Sixteen grey volumes stand down — the retail fixtures and the whole lounge suite
— so the eleven baked garments are finally on the shop floor instead of inside
an opaque box. `qa/pine-hills-v3/v2-retail-floor.png` against `v3-retail-floor.png`:
v2 is a flat grey slab filling the frame, v3 is the stocked shelf unit and the
pegboard wall behind it.

**A trap the first run fell into and everyone should know about:** `main.cjs`
validates `--clubhouse` against its own copy of the variant list, because the
main process cannot import the renderer's ES module. A room added to one list and
not the other is silently ignored and the launch falls back to `modern-public` —
a different building. The first v3 run reported `requested=null layout=null`,
which reads like a broken variant rather than a rejected flag. A test now holds
the two lists together.

## BLOCK 5 — THE FOUR HARDGOODS ARE BAKED, AND THE COUNTER IS IN

`assert_maps.mjs` failed all four first, exactly as you said it would: 119
findings, the counter alone failing twelve times. **All fifteen assets now carry
packed normal, occlusion and metallic-roughness on every mapped surface, and
baked COLOR_0 on every primitive.**

Through the SAME pipeline, not a second one. `weave.wire()` is not about cloth —
it takes a periodic height field and derives the three maps from it and from
nothing else, which is as true of quarter-sawn oak as of a pique knit.
`tools/blender/hero/v7/surface.py` contributes seven height fields and registers
them into weave's own tables, so the counter goes through literally the same
call the polo did. Plus the one thing cloth never needs: a metal channel.

**The tile control earned its keep three times, and twice it was the PICTURE
that caught it:**

* anisotropy 1.02 on oak, 1.01 on brass — weave's lattice is n × n, so the field
  is isotropic by construction and transposing it changes nothing. There was no
  grain in the grain.
* the control then PASSED an oak that looks like leopard print. Contour bands of
  a 2D field close into loops; a ring boundary runs the length of the board.
* and it passed a painted driver crown that rendered PITTED, because an 18 mm
  cell of spray tooth is a dent.

A graphite shaft is not a steel one, so the clubs pass their families explicitly
rather than by name inference — which would have matched "DriverShaft" on
"shaft" and written metallic 1.0 into a composite.

**And the counter is wired, in v3 only.** The grey slab is hidden, not deleted:
it is the shape the colliders, the register stand, the queue head and the ledger
are measured against. `qa/pine-hills-v3/v3-desk.png` at the queue head shows the
cream worktop, the dark oak front and the brass rail. **`pine-hills-v2` is
untouched, and the driver asserts it in both rooms, both ways.**

## BLOCK 6d — THE FRAMING GATE, AND THE VERDICT

You said: fix the gate, get a verdict. Both done, and the verdict is **yes, it
is still there.**

The gate needed two things. The head's projected HEIGHT as a fraction of the
frame (8% minimum; the doorway shots filled a twentieth), and the ANGLE off the
subject's facing — because the first run with the fill gate produced a perfectly
framed BACK OF A HEAD. The subject turns while the camera walks. The camera is
now placed rather than walked, which is a deliberate exception to "no teleports"
and is legitimate because the route was never the thing under test: it was the
thing that kept preventing the test.

`qa/goal33/d6-profile-d6c.png` is the first frame this project has taken of a
face in profile — 90 degrees, 2.2 yd, head filling 8.7% of the frame — and the
brow and the moustache are both dark rectangles standing clear of the skin.

**Why two previous fixes could not work.** Both were right about the number they
measured. Both then seated a flat 52 mm slab against a 155 mm sphere, and a
chord that touches a sphere at its centre stands off it at its ends: buried
1.2 mm under its centre, **16.4 mm clear at its outer top corner**. The temple
end is the one you see in profile. Features are segmented now and pushed along
their own radial until the WORST corner is inside the sphere the renderer
actually draws — 16.4 mm proud to 1.5 mm buried on the brow, 7.8 to 1.5 on the
mouth.

**The test that missed it is rebuilt.** It parsed the source with a regex and
computed the distance to the MIDDLE of the brow's inner face — the one place on
it that was seated. It builds a character and measures every corner now, with
two controls.
